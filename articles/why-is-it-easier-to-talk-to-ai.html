Why Is It Easier to Talk to AI Than to People?

October 2026 · Inside

Have you ever found it easier to tell a chatbot something personal than to tell a friend? You can type your thoughts without worrying about facial expressions, interruptions or an uncomfortable silence.

There may be psychological reasons why this feels easier.

The comfort of a conversation without obvious judgement

Talking about personal problems can feel risky. We may worry about being misunderstood, criticised or becoming a burden to someone else.

A chatbot offers a different experience. It is available on demand and can respond without the visible social reactions we encounter in face-to-face conversations. This may make it feel easier to share sensitive information.

A review of research on self-disclosure to conversational AI, covering 26 studies up to the end of 2022, found that nine reported greater disclosure to conversational technologies than to human interviewers or online questionnaires. One found the opposite and three found no significant difference [1]. The authors suggest that the difference is most likely to appear with sensitive topics, where anonymity and the absence of judgement seem to matter.

This suggests that people may sometimes feel more comfortable opening up to AI, but it is not true for everyone or every situation. Most of these studies took place in research settings, such as interviews and surveys, and before today's chatbots, so they cannot tell us directly how people talk to AI in everyday life.

Does feeling understood mean being understood?

A chatbot can produce supportive language, reflect your concerns and help you organise your thoughts. These responses may feel comforting.

However, a convincing response is not proof that a system understands your experience in the same way a person might. AI can also misunderstand a situation or offer inappropriate advice.

Research published in the Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication in 2021 asked participants recruited at a US university to discuss a personal stressor with either a scripted chatbot or a human partner. In both cases, emotional support worked through how supported people felt, but that link was stronger with human partners than with chatbots [2].

This does not mean that conversations with AI cannot help. It means that the experience of receiving support may differ depending on who—or what—is responding.

A tool, not a replacement

Some people may find AI useful for practising how to express difficult feelings, organising their thoughts or preparing for a challenging conversation. But it should not become your only source of emotional support.

Human relationships involve shared experiences, mutual responsibility and real-world actions that a chatbot cannot provide in the same way.

Perhaps the most useful question is not whether talking to AI is good or bad. It is what role we want it to play in our lives.

If talking to AI feels easier than talking to people, what makes human conversations feel difficult—and what might help you reconnect?
