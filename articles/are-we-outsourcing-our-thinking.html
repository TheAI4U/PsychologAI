Are We Outsourcing Our Thinking to AI?

October 2026 · Utopia

Imagine you have to write an important report. Instead of starting with your own ideas, you ask AI to write it for you. The result looks good, and you finish in minutes. But how much of the thinking did you actually do?

What is cognitive offloading?

Cognitive offloading means using something outside your mind to help with a mental task. Writing a reminder, using a calculator or checking a map are everyday examples.

AI takes this further. It can summarise information, suggest arguments, explain difficult concepts and produce complete drafts. These abilities can save time and help people work with unfamiliar subjects.

But there is an important distinction between using AI to support your thinking and allowing it to replace your thinking.

What does research tell us?

A study presented at the 2025 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems surveyed 319 knowledge workers, people whose jobs involve working with information, who used generative AI at least weekly, about how they think critically when using it [1].

The researchers found that greater confidence in AI was associated with less reported critical-thinking effort, while greater confidence in one's own abilities was associated with more. Participants also described critical thinking as shifting towards checking AI-generated information, combining responses with their own work and supervising the task.

This is an important finding, but it has limits. The study relied on a survey and on participants' own reports, so it can show associations but not cause and effect. It does not show that using AI permanently reduces anyone's ability to think, or that everyone who uses AI thinks less.

How can you use AI without switching off your brain?

Try changing the order in which you work. These are practical suggestions rather than findings from the study:

  • Think first. Write down your initial ideas before asking AI for help.
  • Question the answer. Look for missing information, weak arguments and unsupported claims.
  • Verify important facts. Check reliable sources instead of assuming a confident answer is correct.
  • Make the final decision yourself. Use AI to explore possibilities, not to remove your responsibility for choosing.

AI can be a useful thinking partner, but convenience should not be confused with understanding.

The goal is not to do everything without technology. It is to remain capable of thinking critically when technology is available.

If AI gives you the answer, can you still explain why that answer makes sense?
