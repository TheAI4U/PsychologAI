The Future: AI and Us or AI vs. Us?

Artificial intelligence is changing much more than the way we do things. Little by little, it may also be transforming how we think, how we relate to others, and even how we see ourselves.

On this site, we invite you to explore three aspects that are more connected than they appear: our mind, the risks, and the opportunities that AI brings.

We are not neutral about AI. We have to be part of the change.

Inside

Every tool we use changes the way we think. Google Maps made navigation easier, but it also means we rely less on our own memory and sense of direction. AI takes this further: it can remember, create, solve problems and even help us make decisions. What happens when we stop doing some of these things for ourselves?

🧩

Cognitive Dependence

AI can remember information, solve problems and make decisions in seconds. The more we rely on it, the less often we may practise those mental processes ourselves. Are we becoming more efficient, or simply doing less thinking?

💭

Emotional Dependence

People are increasingly using AI to explore emotions, personal problems and difficult decisions. It can offer a space to reflect without judgement, but what happens when a system always listens, never tires, and is designed to respond to us? Are we starting to replace human conversation with a machine?

🎨

Creativity

AI can help us generate ideas, images and possibilities that we might not have reached alone. But when millions of people draw from the same tools and patterns, could greater creative possibility also lead to less originality?

👁️

Reality and Trust

With the rise of AI-generated videos, images and cloned voices, how do we know whether what we see, hear or read was created by a person or a machine?

Utopia

AI can also augment human capabilities. The same technology that raises real risks can widen access, support learning and free up time.

📚

Personalised learning

A patient tutor available at any hour, adapting to the pace of the person learning.

♿

Accessibility

Transcription, translation, description and voice tools that open doors for people who were previously excluded.

🔬

Research and health

Pattern-finding at a scale humans cannot manage, supporting diagnosis and discovery.

🤝

Support, not substitute

Mental health tools can complement professional care. They are not a replacement for a qualified human.

⏳

Time

Automating repetitive work can return hours to the things only people can do.

🌍

Connection

Breaking down language barriers and helping people communicate who otherwise could not.

Does AI make us less human, or give us more time to be human?

Soft Dystopia

Imagined Futures

Dystopia is a thought experiment, not a prediction. Each scenario below asks a different question about where a trend could lead if nobody intervened.

  • The comfortable fog. Everyone has a personal assistant that answers everything, so no one ever has to sit with an unanswered question.
  • The echo chamber. Every conversation is tailored so precisely that we never encounter an idea that genuinely challenges us.
  • The invisible editor. We stop being able to tell which of our own opinions we actually formed.
  • The substitute. AI becomes the first place people go for comfort, and human relationships become optional.

Beyond

What do we do with what we know?

The Choice

Assistance and dependence are not the same thing. Read each situation below and ask yourself honestly: would you hand this over?

  • Would you let AI choose your next career?
  • Would you let AI write a difficult personal message for you?
  • Would you let AI decide what news you should read?
  • Would you let AI recommend who you should date?
  • Would you let AI help you make a medical decision?
  • Would you let AI be your first source of emotional support?

There is no score and no diagnosis. The point is simply to notice where your own line sits, and whether it moves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI really understand how I feel?

No. AI can recognise patterns in language and respond in ways that feel empathetic, but it has no feelings, no lived experience, and no real understanding. It can be a useful sounding board, but it is not a substitute for a person who knows you.

Is using AI for emotional support a bad thing?

Not automatically. Many people find it helpful for organising their thoughts or talking through a problem at 3 a.m. The risk appears when it becomes the only place someone goes, or when it replaces professional care that is actually needed.

Does AI make us less creative?

It can go either way. Used as a tool, it can help us explore ideas faster and try things we would not have tried alone. Used as a shortcut, it can nudge everyone toward the same average answer. The difference is in how we use it.

Should I worry about becoming dependent on AI?

It is worth paying attention to. Dependence usually creeps in quietly: you start by asking occasionally, then regularly, then always. A useful question to ask yourself: could I still do this without it, if I had to?
